01 Start here: the project in two minutes

PeptideLab generates peptide sequences, predicts antimicrobial activity and toxicity, and ranks candidates through a dashboard. It also predicts existing dataset peptides and sequences supplied by the user.

A peptide is a short chain of amino acids. We write it as letters, for example KWKLFKKIGAVLKVL. Some peptides can inhibit microorganisms. These are antimicrobial peptides (AMPs). A useful screening candidate should have high predicted antimicrobial activity and low predicted toxicity.

Everyday analogy: imagine a recipe writer, two reviewers and a sorting assistant. The VAE writes new “recipes” (sequences). One reviewer scores antimicrobial activity. Another scores toxicity. The sorting assistant combines the scores. The dashboard lets us use and inspect the whole process.

The outcome is a computational shortlist for further investigation. Laboratory experiments would be needed to establish real activity and safety.

How to read: read sections 1–6 for the big picture, 7–17 for the models and results, 18–23 for dashboard use, and the remaining sections for code, running instructions and viva preparation. All examples labelled “illustrative” are teaching examples, not experimental results.

02 The problem, inputs and outputs

There are too many possible amino-acid combinations to test every sequence in a laboratory. Our project helps organize the search.

QuestionOur answer
Can we propose sequences?Yes, using a trained LSTM VAE.
Can we screen existing sequences?Yes, from datasets, pasted text or uploaded files.
What is predicted?Binary antimicrobial-class membership and a dataset-specific toxicity class.
What is returned?Scores, class labels, property-screen flags, ranks, descriptors and downloadable CSVs.
What is outside the demonstrated scope?Exact MIC values, clinical efficacy, comprehensive safety, laboratory validation and clinical use.

Example input: one peptide sequence. Example output: activity score, toxicity score and rank among the sequences in that batch. Scores are model support values, not calibrated biological probabilities.

03 Complete workflow: three entry routes, one screening system

There are three entry routes:

  1. Generate: VAE creates sequences, then sequence filters are applied before prediction.
  2. Dataset: choose existing peptides and run both predictors.
  3. Custom: paste or upload sequences and run both predictors.

The generated-sequence novelty and repetition filters belong to generation. Dataset and custom screening accept supported input sequences, including known ones. They do not require every input to be novel.

After prediction, an additional property-screen flag is calculated and all screened rows are ranked. Each result records the prediction engine and ranking settings.

05 Cleaning data and avoiding accidental leakage

  1. Standardize sequence formatting and case.
  2. Check supported amino-acid letters.
  3. Apply the relevant length limits.
  4. Remove exact duplicates and handle conflicting classifier labels.
  5. Prevent identical sequences from crossing splits within each task.
  6. Fit feature scalers using training data only.
  7. Keep source provenance and checksums.

Supported letters: ACDEFGHIKLMNPQRSTVWY. For example, acdef ghik normalizes to ACDEFGHIK; unsupported letters are rejected rather than silently guessed.

Leakage means information reaches a model or selection process in a way that makes evaluation easier than intended. Exact-duplicate removal helps, but related, nonidentical sequences can still cross splits. Our similarity audit measures that remaining overlap.

07 How letters become model inputs

Computers need numbers. The project uses three kinds of representation:

RepresentationSimple explanation
TokensAssign each amino acid an integer ID. Padding fills unused positions in a batch.
Learned embeddingsLook up a numerical vector for each token. Training changes these vectors.
DescriptorsCalculate understandable properties directly from the sequence.

The 25 descriptors are 20 amino-acid fractions plus length, charge proxy, hydrophobic fraction, mean hydropathy and entropy.

Descriptor example: for KKRDD, length = 5, lysine fraction = 2/5 = 0.4 and charge proxy = K + R − D − E = 2 + 1 − 2 − 0 = +1. This charge proxy is a rough count, not a full pH-dependent chemical calculation.

Hydrophobic fraction counts the project’s chosen hydrophobic residues. Mean hydropathy averages fixed residue values. Entropy describes residue diversity: a repeated single letter has low entropy, while a varied sequence generally has higher entropy.

08 The generator: understand the LSTM VAE

LSTM means Long Short-Term Memory: a neural network designed to process ordered sequences. VAE means Variational Autoencoder.

  1. The encoder reads a peptide.
  2. It produces a mean and log variance for a latent distribution.
  3. We sample a latent vector z.
  4. The decoder uses z and previous residues to predict the next residue.
  5. During new generation, we sample z from a standard normal distribution and decode without an input peptide.

Reparameterization is z = mean + exp(0.5 × log_variance) × noise. It allows randomness while still training the encoder through gradients.

Think of the latent vector as a compact numerical recipe description. It is not a manually named list such as “activity, charge, toxicity.” Those meanings are not assigned to individual latent dimensions.
SettingDeployed value
Embedding dimension32
LSTM hidden dimension96
Latent dimension24
Word dropout0.2
Completed full-data epochs30
KL weightWarmup over 10 epochs to 0.02

The latent vector initializes decoder state and is supplied at each decoding step. The VAE learns sequence patterns; its training objective does not directly optimize the downstream activity/toxicity scores.

09 VAE training: losses, dropout and checkpoints

Total loss = reconstruction cross-entropy + beta × KL divergence

  • Reconstruction: penalizes incorrect next-residue predictions; lower is better.
  • KL: encourages the latent distribution to stay near a standard normal distribution so sampling is possible.
  • Beta: starts small and reaches 0.02, gradually introducing regularization.
  • Word dropout: removes some decoder input-token information during training to encourage use of the latent vector.

Adam updates VAE weights. A checkpoint is a saved copy of model parameters. Checkpoint selection compares validation reconstruction plus fixed 0.02 × KL across epochs, avoiding a changing selection target during warmup. Plotted epoch objectives can instead use each epoch’s actual beta; read the chart label.

Teacher forcing means supplying true previous tokens while predicting the next one. Free running means supplying the model’s own previous predictions. Free running is harder because mistakes can accumulate.

10 Sampling: seed, temperature and sequence filters

Seed: controls the random-number sequence. The same model and settings reproduce a generation run in the tested environment. Temperature: changes the spread of next-token choices. Lower values favour more likely choices; higher values allow more variation. Higher is not automatically better.

The decoder suppresses padding/start tokens, forbids END before five residues and caps generated length at 60. This constrains valid output; it does not prove biological quality.

Generation rejects candidates in this order: truncated output → outside selected length range → low complexity/repetition → exact known match → duplicate in the accepted pool. Each rejected sequence receives one reason in the audit.

Low-complexity checks use entropy below 2.0 bits or a single residue making up more than half the sequence. Known-match checks cover GRAMPA and classifier training pools.

If you request 500 raw samples and 420 pass, the application predicts and ranks 420 sequences. Requesting 500 samples does not promise 500 accepted candidates.

11 Compact predictor architecture: CNN plus descriptors

A convolution slides a small learned pattern detector over a sequence. Kernels of lengths 3, 5 and 7 inspect different local contexts. Global max pooling keeps the strongest response from each filter while ignoring padded positions.

The 96 pooled responses are combined with 25 descriptors, giving 121 input features to a 48-unit dense layer. ReLU adds nonlinearity, dropout 0.3 regularizes training, and a final sigmoid maps the output to 0–1.

The separate random forest uses 240 decision trees and the 25 descriptors. A forest combines many decision trees to learn nonlinear patterns. Class weighting helps account for unequal positive/negative counts. The CNN uses class-weighted binary cross-entropy and AdamW.

For each task, validation ROC-AUC chooses CNN, forest or their equal-weight average. The deployed selection is activity = ensemble; toxicity = CNN. Activity and toxicity models do not share trained task weights.

Illustrative ensemble calculation: if CNN activity = 0.8 and forest activity = 0.6, the ensemble activity score is (0.8 + 0.6)/2 = 0.7.

12 ESM-2 alternative: a pretrained protein representation

ESM-2 learned protein sequence representations before our project. Our small backbone is facebook/esm2_t6_8M_UR50D. It is stored locally with its source revision, checksums and license.

  1. Tokenize each sequence.
  2. Run the frozen transformer.
  3. Average last-layer residue embeddings, excluding special tokens and padding.
  4. Scale the 320 features with a scaler fitted only on training examples.
  5. Train independent task heads: Conv1D(32 filters, kernel 3) → ReLU → max pooling → dense 64 → dropout 0.15 → one score.

Frozen means our task training does not change the backbone. A sequence-keyed embedding cache speeds repeated predictions. New sequences receive newly computed embeddings. Cache checks help prevent mismatched artifacts.

The head convolves over the pooled embedding features, not residue positions. This is an explicit adaptation of pretrained-representation + CNN modelling, not an exact UniDL4BioPep reproduction. Validation checkpoint selection uses AUC, with a 30-epoch maximum and patience 6.

The compact models remain the default because validation AUC favours them for both tasks. You can still select ESM-2 throughout dataset, custom and generated predictions.

14 Ranking rules and activity weight, with worked examples

Product: priority = 100 × A × (1 − T), where A is activity score and T is toxicity score. It rewards the combination of high activity and low toxicity.

Weighted difference: S = w × A − (1 − w) × T. For display, priority = 100 × [S + (1 − w)], equivalent to 100 × [wA + (1−w)(1−T)]. The shift and scale preserve the ordering.

Illustrative peptideATProductWeighted w=0.7Weighted w=0.3
P0.950.3066.5087.5077.50
Q0.800.0576.0084.5090.50

With more activity emphasis (0.7), P wins. With more emphasis on reducing toxicity (0.3), Q wins. Product also favours Q here.

Choose preferences before running a prediction. Weights do not change the trained model scores. Priority is not a probability; 95/100 does not mean 95% biological success. Equal priorities use sequence ordering as a deterministic tie-breaker. Ranks are local to each batch.

16 Evaluation metrics explained with one small example

For activity, “positive” means antimicrobial. For toxicity, “positive” means toxic.

Illustrative confusion matrixPredicted negativePredicted positive
Actually negativeTN = 8FP = 2
Actually positiveFN = 1TP = 9
  • Accuracy: correct/total = (8+9)/20 = 85%.
  • Precision: TP/(TP+FP) = 9/11 = 81.8%. Of predicted positives, how many are correct?
  • Recall: TP/(TP+FN) = 9/10 = 90%. Of actual positives, how many are found?
  • F1: harmonic mean of precision and recall = 85.7% here.
  • ROC-AUC: how well scores order positives above negatives across thresholds. Roughly 0.5 is random ordering; 1.0 is perfect.
  • MCC: agreement accounting for all four confusion-matrix cells; 1 is perfect, 0 indicates no correlation and −1 indicates complete inverse agreement.
  • Balanced accuracy: average of sensitivity and specificity, helping expose misleading raw accuracy with class imbalance.

A confidence interval summarizes sampling uncertainty under stated assumptions. Our stratified test bootstraps do not include all uncertainty from retraining, peptide families or new datasets. Model disagreement is the absolute CNN–forest score difference, not a calibrated confidence interval. Feature importance describes associations, not biological causation.

24 Research studies, limitations and honest conclusions

The 22 small VAE experiments use 200-sequence subsets split 160/20/20 and 30 epochs. Ten seeds test the default. Three matched seeds compare other latent sizes and word dropout. These are new controlled studies, not recovered missing historical notebooks or proof of globally optimal hyperparameters.

Exhaustive normalized edit similarity is 1 − edit_distance / max(sequence_lengths). At cutoff 0.8, training matches occur for 943/8877 activity test examples, 118/580 toxicity examples and 421/651 VAE examples. Below-cutoff matches are not stored in the high-similarity CSV. This is not BLAST identity, a homology guarantee or a similarity-clustered split.

Activity negatives and positives have different length distributions. A length-only baseline has high activity AUC (about 0.967). The 5–40-residue activity test subset contains 1,809 positives but only 8 negatives; raw accuracy near 99.7% hides balanced accuracy of 0.625. This limits claims about diverse short non-AMP discrimination.

Other boundaries: no laboratory experiments; organism models classify MIC at a 10 µM cutoff and do not estimate an exact concentration; no assay-specific comprehensive safety; scores remain uncalibrated; novelty is exact-match novelty within checked pools. The valid conclusion is that we implemented and evaluated a computational generation/screening workflow on stated data.

26 Training versus using the finished application

Training: reads labelled examples, computes losses, updates parameters, uses validation for selection and saves artifacts. It takes longer and can change results.

Inference: loads the saved artifacts and computes outputs for inputs. Clicking Predict or Generate is inference. Normal demonstration does not need retraining.

The app caches loaded models. ESM also caches embeddings for known sequences. Local model files enable offline inference after dependencies are installed. New ESM sequences need CPU computation, not an online prediction API.

Recorded direct dependency versions are in requirements-lock.txt. This is a direct-dependency record, not a full transitive environment lock. Use the recorded scikit-learn version for the included forest artifacts.

30 Viva questions with short answers

Why use a VAE?
It learns a distribution of sequence representations from which we can sample new sequences.
Why LSTM?
A peptide is an ordered sequence, and an LSTM can use sequence context while encoding or decoding.
Why not generate random letters?
The VAE learns patterns from training sequences; random letters do not use those learned patterns. Neither method alone proves biological function.
Does the VAE ensure antimicrobial activity?
No. Separate predictors screen its outputs.
Why two task models?
Antimicrobial activity and toxicity are different properties with different labels.
Why use CNNs?
The compact CNN learns local sequence patterns at several context sizes.
Why a random forest?
It is a useful nonlinear descriptor baseline and can be combined with the CNN.
Why compare ESM?
Pretrained protein representations may transfer useful information; we measure whether they help on our data.
Why is ESM not the default?
Compact models achieved higher validation AUC for both tasks.
What does a score of 0.9 mean?
Strong model support for a class; it is not a verified 90% biological probability.
Why different class and property thresholds?
Class evaluation uses 0.5; the separate screening rule expresses a more selective follow-up decision.
What is novelty?
No exact match within specified reference pools. It does not prove global originality or low similarity.
What is posterior collapse?
A VAE can learn to ignore its latent representation. We inspect KL behaviour and latent interventions rather than assuming it cannot happen.
Why report multiple metrics?
Accuracy can hide class imbalance. Precision, recall, F1, AUC, MCC and confusion matrices reveal different behaviour.
What is the biggest scientific limitation?
No experimental validation, together with benchmark length bias and related sequences across splits.
What is our contribution?
An integrated, reproducible computational application with generation, two-task screening, model comparison, explicit ranking, exports and research diagnostics.
Did we reproduce every referenced paper?
No. We use documented architectural adaptations and published data, with our own measured results.
Was AI used?
AI assisted implementation, testing and documentation. The team should understand and verify the work and follow the course disclosure rules.
